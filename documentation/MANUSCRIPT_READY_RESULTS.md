# Manuscript-Ready Results Language

## Main result
In the preferred linear probability model with player and team-season fixed effects, full pregame controls, and standard errors clustered by player and game, the post-PPP star × advance-national-TV interaction was **3.15 percentage points** (95% CI 0.57 to 5.73; p = 0.0167). This estimate represents a larger post-policy change in new absence-onset probability for stars in advance-announced national-TV games relative to the corresponding changes for non-stars and non-national-TV games.

## Measurement sensitivity
The estimate was sensitive to the construction of national-TV exposure and injury-report timing. Under the harmonized national-TV definition, the preferred estimate was **1.77 percentage points** (95% CI -0.80 to 4.35; p = 0.177). Reconstructing outcomes from common 1:30, 5:30, and 8:30 ET injury-report snapshots produced a legacy-TV estimate of **2.63 percentage points** (95% CI 0.05 to 5.22; p = 0.046), while combining harmonized report timing with harmonized TV produced an estimate of **1.24 percentage points** (95% CI -1.35 to 3.82; p = 0.347).

## Identification diagnostics
The legacy estimate remained positive in leave-one-season-out analyses and after adding player-specific linear trends. A joint pre-trend test did not reject equality of the pre-policy interaction coefficients (p ≈ 0.433), and placebo policy dates within the pre-period were not significant. However, a stratified game-level TV permutation placebo yielded a two-sided randomization-style p-value of **0.09** for the legacy TV definition and **0.26** for the harmonized definition. Because national-TV assignment is observational, these permutation exercises are falsification diagnostics rather than exact randomization inference.

## Label mechanism
Conditional on new injury-labeled onsets, the estimated post-PPP star × TV change in the probability of a vague rather than specific label was **-8.42 percentage points** (95% CI -23.96 to 7.12; p = 0.288). The stronger player + team-season fixed-effect specification was similarly null. Nonlinear and multinomial checks likewise did not reveal a distinctive shift toward vague labels.

## Heterogeneity
Predeclared tests by back-to-back status, opponent strength, recent workload, and proximity to the 65-game threshold yielded no statistically detectable heterogeneity after multiplicity adjustment.

## Discussion sentence
Taken together, the estimates are consistent with a change in differential star absence behavior under the original advance-TV construction, but the measurement sensitivity and null label-mechanism results do not support a strong causal interpretation or a claim that teams increasingly disguised rest using vague injury language.
